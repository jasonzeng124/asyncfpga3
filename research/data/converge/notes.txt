2. HOW FAR A RE-ROUTE MOVES A MARGIN (local loops that kept every route's corner_audit report)
Data: every $RUNS/ca/<loop>_<I>/rule_<C>/report.txt of /tmp/bot/runs and the subagents' runs: 24 loops, 97 pairs of
consecutive routes, 14958 constraint-corner pairs; tools/rescatter.py -> rescatter.txt. A model statement is marked so.
- Constraints none of whose lines changed size between the two routes (n 11877): the margin moves by sd 483 ps,
  median |d| 283 ps (p5 -792, p10 -580, p90 +574). By rule: H sd 412, F 491, A 508, E 534, I 616.
- Raise-only pairs (56 routes, 112 route-corners): 1.54 new short rows per route and corner. They had 0-2162 ps
  (median 474) on the route before and moved by -52 .. -2651 ps (median -596); 9 of the 172 had one of their own lines
  raised. P(short on the next route | margin now): 0.07 in [0,100), 0.13 [100,200), 0.08 [200,300), 0.07 [300,500),
  0.04 [500,1000), 0.004 above; a route and corner holds 1.2 / 2.0 / 2.2 / 4.4 / 13.6 / 53 such constraints.
- Persistence (3 consecutive routes, no line of the constraint changed, n 3841): corr(|d1|, |d2|) = 0.31; |d2| median
  414 ps after |d1| > 500, 238 ps after |d1| < 150. Some constraints are jumpier than others, not by much.
- At identical sizes (run A below: the seed-S route and two more seeds per finish step) the short sets barely overlap:
  collatz64 s3 it 5: 10 distinct short constraints over 3 draws, none short on two; ipow s3 it 5: 13, none; xorshift s1
  it 5: 12, one (A'm umux1) short on all three; sqrtsub s2 it 6: 2, ucontrol_merge1's A on all three.
So a finish route is a fresh draw of a few hundred constraints' margins, each moving by ~0.5 ns; what goes short is
mostly not what was short, nor what was near (the rows came from a median 474 ps).

3. WHAT EACH CHANGE COULD REACH
(a) More finish routes: the clean rate per raise-only step rises, 7 -> 11 -> 21 %: each step pads what it found short.
    Beyond step 3 the farm has no data; tested locally (run B).
(b) Headroom 2 instead of 1: it acts on lines raised in the finish phase only: 112 of 2367 rows (4.7 %) are on such
    lines, and on 0 of 383 routes would it have removed every row (every row a recurrence within one element: 0).
(c) Headroom proportional to a line's measured movement: on raised lines, the same 112 rows; on EVERY line it is a
    slack. Model on the raise-only pairs (a constraint at a < X padded to X shifts its next margin by X - a): new short
    rows per route and corner 1.54 (X 0) -> 1.50 (100), 1.34 (300), 1.04 (500), 0.63 (700), 0.36 (1000). Its cost,
    measured on one route (su_xorshift_1_5 at corner 0.31: corner_audit --slack X, then ratchet.py --raise-only
    --headroom 1 from sizes_5): kernel delay elements 100 -> 108 at slack 0 (the loop as is), 134 at 500, 186 at 1000.
    A per-step slack pays ~1 % fewer new rows per added element; the plain raise-only step ~2.5 % (+8 elements for a
    step from ~2.7 to ~2.2 expected rows). Not tested routed.
(d) More draws per finish step (AUTO_FINISH_SEEDS, implemented): no padding at all; each extra route is a fresh draw at
    the same sizes, and the loop ships the first one the rule finds clean. Tested locally (runs A, A2, B).

4c. WHY ONE MORE SEED-S STEP BEATS DRAWS ON NEW SEEDS (runs A / A2 reports; seed-S vs S+1000 j at the same sizes)
- Margin scatter per constraint is the same for a new seed at the same sizes (sd 487 ps, n 6646) as for the next
  iteration on the same seed with none of the constraint's lines changed (sd 482, n 2677): no seed "family" in sd.
- But the lower tail differs: constraints short on the seed-S route 1.97 % vs 3.20 % on the other seeds (ratio 1.63;
  below 200 / 500 / 1000 ps the ratio is only 1.13 / 1.16 / 1.05). Rows: 74 over 19 seed-S routes (3.9 each) vs 297
  over 34 alternates (8.7 each). The raise-only steps pad what went short on that seed's own routes; another seed's
  short rows are mostly others, not yet padded.
- Outcome: 2 of 40 alternate draws CLEAN (5 %: runs A 2 / 27, A2 0 / 13), against 4 of 5 loops CLEAN on their first
  extra seed-S step (run B / C). A2 (draws, it 5-7) was stopped at the 2 h job limit after 13 draws, none clean:
  branchloop s1 9 / 14, 24 / 10, 10 rows; varshift s4 19 / 10, 27 / 6; iterative_division s2 13 / 20 (seed-S routes 6,
  11 / 4, 10 / 1 rows).
So the cheapest lever is AUTO_FINISH (more raise-only steps on the loop's own seed); AUTO_FINISH_SEEDS is a fallback
that sometimes finds a clean route earlier (xorshift s1, collatz64 s3) at ~20 extra routes per clean one.

6. CONCLUSIONS, AND WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
- Why NOT CLEAN: a re-route moves every margin by ~0.5 ns (sd), the finish phase pads only what was short on the
  route before, and what goes short next is mostly what had 0.2-0.8 ns. Headroom on the raised lines (b, c) cannot
  reach it; a slack over every line can, at +34 / +86 % delay elements for slack 0.5 / 1 ns (one route).
- Cheapest lever on loops with few rows: more raise-only steps on the loop's own seed (AUTO_FINISH, an existing knob):
  4 of 5 CLEAN on the first extra route, +2..+3 elements, P within route scatter. On loops with 4-14 rows per route it
  did not converge in three more steps (0 of 3) and added 11-29 elements.
- AUTO_FINISH_SEEDS (implemented; draws on new seeds): 2 of 40 draws CLEAN; it rescued 2 of 8 loops early (one that
  more steps did not, collatz64 s3) at 3-6 extra routes; new seeds carry about twice the seed-S rows.
- AUTO_SHIP_LEAST (implemented): on about half of the NOT CLEAN loops a less short route was built than the one
  shipped, and on ringfuse1/31 that route passes all eight draws where the shipped one fails one. Cheap, general.
- Suggested default after farm finseeds1: AUTO_FINISH=6 with AUTO_SHIP_LEAST=1; AUTO_FINISH_SEEDS as an opt-in.
Not shown: n is small (8 loops, 8 kernels; one route per loop and lever), and P differences are within route scatter;
the clean routes of runs B / C were not judged with draws here (full stack EQUIVALENT and ffmux-clean only; xorshift s1's
adopted route passed 2 of 2 draws); whether least-short routes fail fewer draws in general rests on one case (only 2 of
the farm's NOT CLEAN routes failed any draw); the slack variant was not routed; nothing here reduces the scatter itself
(an incremental placement keeping the previous route's cells where they were would, and is the open lead).
