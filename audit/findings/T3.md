### 1.2 Inputs
`Env` (Spec.lean:309-314) requires `va k, vb k < 2^31` and (EnvIn, :286-295) that `busVal x B.a.data` equals `va k`
while the k-th request/ack is up. In the target `a.data` lists 32 entries of which 19 are distinct nodes and a[31]
a constant tie (BOUNDARY.md), so for a pair whose bits disagree with the merge, no behaviour satisfies Env and
NetCorrect says nothing about it (vacuously true). This restriction is STATED, in the §7 prose ("For any other pair
the theorem says nothing: this bitstream cannot be offered it"), but not as a named predicate in any definition, and
it is not in STATUS.md's hypothesis list. Plain answer: the statement covers exactly the pairs the target's wiring
can represent (the 16 table vectors among them), and silently nothing else; zero operands are allowed
(Nat.gcd 0 0 = 0, as in T1).

### 1.3 Safety, liveness, delays
- Safety: CircIn/CircOut (:318-326) give four-phase moves on all five channels; Correct (:334-349) gives out0's k-th
  handshake data = `Nat.gcd (va k) (vb k)` from Δo after its rise while ack is low, and at most n rises. In order:
  yes (k = rises so far).
- Liveness: AckLive on a, b, start; ReqLive on out0/end with `Offered n`; for finite n, all boundary nodes eventually
  low and exactly m rises. Yes.
- Delays: `Behaves` (:170) = ∃ one corner c ∈ [0,1], every gate `GateOK`; `Adm` (:142) = [FAST_MIN/g, SLOW_MAX·g] ∩
  scatter κ around lerp(c). Since Behaves is a hypothesis, the statement covers every c and every admissible
  per-pin, per-time read map (time-varying transport and pulse swallowing). Delays are whole ps (Nat), per pin not
  per edge (stated). Involution channels are not covered (item 3a).

### 1.4 Pipelined environment and which theorem is "THE theorem"
`Env` alone allows pipelining (each channel's ReqMoves only looks at its own ack; with the shared request it is the
fork of all three acks). `NetCorrect` (:351) is the owner's statement. `NetCorrectSeq` (:365) is a SEPARATE
definition with one extra binder `SeqEnv x B R` (:360: the k+1-th input request rises only after out0's ack rose k
times and out0 req and ack are both low). Spec §7 (:409) now writes "**THE theorem**: decode ... ∧ NetCorrectSeq ...,
and the refutation ... ¬NetCorrect ... (for every T, at g = 1)". PROGRAM.md "THE goal": "The environment is not
sequential ... The theorem must cover the general one ... If it is not harmless, that is the counterexample." So
under PROGRAM.md THE theorem is NetCorrect, and the deliverable, given T2's GLS, is its refutation. NetCorrectSeq is a
weaker, additional theorem whose extra hypothesis is not one of the five; STATUS.md row 5's "allowed (SeqEnv: a
restriction of the owner's env, forced by the counterexample)" is OVERSTATED: a restriction forced by a
counterexample is the counterexample's consequence, not an allowed hypothesis.

### 1.5 Hypotheses as Lean binders
`NetCorrect N B A p T R su Δo := ∀ x n va vb, Behaves p (circuit N B) x → ArbOK T A x → Env x B R su Δo n va vb →
Correct ...`. Binders vs PROGRAM's five:
- `Behaves` = H_PRIM + H_DELAY(g, κ): matches (with the involution gap, 3a).
- `ArbOK T A` = H_ARB(T): the wording matches, the content does not (item 2): it forbids q moving more than T after
  a REQUEST edge, which (with GateOK) bounds the server's hold time, a property of the circuit and, through out0, of
  the env. Also `A` comes out of `decode`, and NOTHING in the statement constrains it (no check that `a.q` is
  driven by an arbiter LUT reading `a.r1, a.r2`): any extra triple in `A` adds an arbitrary "q is quiet" assumption.
  H_DECODE (prjxray bits) does not cover the choice of arbiters.
- `Env` = H_ENV, plus three harness timing parameters: `R` (rst high on exactly [0,R), protocol from R: one stated R
  covers only harnesses with exactly that reset length, not "at least R"), `su` (data set up su ps before the
  request rise), `Δo` (out0/end ack no earlier than Δo after the request rose). PROGRAM.md: H_ENV is four-phase "with
  no timing assumption on it". `su`, `Δo` and the length of reset are timing assumptions on the harness; the Spec
  states them openly (header and EnvIn/EnvOut docstrings) but STATUS.md files them under "allowed". They need an
  owner decision (T1 already raised Δo; still open).
- `SeqEnv` (NetCorrectSeq only): not one of the five.
- `N.WF`, `B.OK N` are conclusions of THE theorem (good), not hypotheses.

### 2.3 My own checks (audit/lean/T3/, all against the frozen `Formal.Target.Spec`, no copies)
1. `ArbOKChar.lean`, `arbOK_iff`: `ArbOK T A x` ⟺ for every listed arbiter, every change of q at a step

   bound for a race; it forbids every q move caused by any other pin of the state gate (A0, rst) more than T after

3. Joint satisfiability (the general question): yes, Behaves ∧ ArbOK ∧ Env can hold together on a netlist with a

### 2.4 Consequence for the target
- Runs NetCorrect / NetCorrectSeq say nothing about: every run in which some listed arbiter's q changes more than T
  after its last request edge. In the held bd_arbiter that is (i) every handover (client 1 released while the server
  holds A0, client 2 waiting) whose server return-to-zero takes ≥ T - d; (ii) for T below q's own response time,
  every run in which any arbiter decision flips; (iii) a reset release with a request high.
- Which T is meaningful: larger T is the weaker hypothesis (stronger theorem). For handovers to be covered, T must
  exceed the longest server wait at any handover. B's simulations of gcd_hw (JOURNAL-B "correction 12:31", a unit-
  delay model, not the target route) show handovers at arbiters 171 and 180 with ONE pair in flight, so this is the
  normal sequential case, not only pipelining. If any handover's A0 fall waits on out0's acknowledge, its wait is set
  by the environment and H_ENV does not bound it: then for every T the frozen ArbOK excludes the slow-env runs,
  i.e. it acts as a timing assumption on the harness, which PROGRAM.md forbids. (Whether such a handover exists on
  the target: not checked; needs the decoded net.)
- Is NetCorrect provable vacuously for the target? Not wholly in general (runs with every q flip close to a request
  edge survive, e.g. n = some 0). But for T below the shortest handover wait, every run that passes a handover is
  excluded; if every representable pair's computation passes one, NetCorrectSeq holds without the circuit computing
  gcd. My toy shows this exact mechanism end to end in the frozen definitions: NetCorrect true for a circuit that
  never answers.
- The other direction: A's §7 plan "¬NetCorrect ... (for every T, at g = 1)". A refuting run must satisfy ArbOK T.
  T2's reordering run has pair 2 waiting at the loop's entry while pair 1 circulates (a long-wait handover), so for
  small T it is excluded; "for every T" is unlikely to be provable with that run. UNVERIFIED (needs the net), but
  the plan as worded is at risk.
- Squeeze: for safety B's `one_ack` needs q settled before A0 rises, i.e. T ≤ the shortest server path after a race;
  for non-vacuity the frozen ArbOK needs T ≥ the longest handover wait. On gcd_hw the first is a few gate delays and
  the second at least a loop iteration, so no single T gives a statement that is both non-vacuous on handovers and
  not open to the double-ack counterexample (3b). B's repair (`ArbOK2`: all non-feedback pins of the state LUT,
  so A0's fall restarts the clock) removes the vacuity half; it is a change to a frozen definition and needs A's
  journaled reason and an audit row.

## 3. H_PRIM and H_ARB as frozen: B's HPrimInv and ArbT

### 3a. HPrimInv (1ed36c34, formal/lean/Formal/Cells/HPrimInv.lean)
Claim (JOURNAL-B 07:41): "read-time gate models (Spec v1 GateOK, WinH) do not cover involution channels (ratPair
counterexample, band [3/2,2]); covered with separated inputs." Lean, plainly: `WinR lo hi x y` = "if the input is
constantly c on [τ - hi, τ - lo] then y(τ) = c"; every read-time model implies it (`win_of_readTime`). `rat_not_win`:
Core's involution pair ratPair (δ∞ = 2, δmin = 1), input pulse [0, 3/2): at τ = 29/10 the input is 1 on the whole
window [9/10, 7/5] for the band [3/2, 2] but the output is 0, so no read map reproduces it (`rat_not_readTime`).
`inv_sep_win`: with input edges more than δ∞ - δmin apart, the involution output is a window behaviour.
Own check: read the statements; axioms printed (Runs). Not re-derived. Meaning: the frozen GateOK (per-pin read
times) covers transport (fixed or varying) and pulse swallowing, but not involution channels whose short-pulse
trailing edges come earlier than the band's lower end. Since Behaves is a hypothesis, this is a coverage gap (the
theorem says nothing about such silicon), not vacuity. PROGRAM.md H_PRIM asks for the pessimistic semantics or all
three; the frozen Spec's "What this does NOT cover" does not list involution. Caveat: B's example is real time and
a chosen band; nothing says the fabric's channels are like ratPair. Verdict: CONFIRMED as a scope statement; the
Spec should name it.

### 3b. ArbT double_ack (6042ffe4, formal/lean/Formal/Cells/ArbT.lean)
Claim: "settled q gives one ack per server transaction; double_ack counterexample when q is unresolved at A0's rise
(T >= 7 vs server latency 6)". Lean, plainly: in B's own `RGate` (the frozen GateOK minus monotone reads; B's trace
reads are monotone, `cex_reads_mono`), the six bd_arbiter gates with their real INITs, unit delays and a [2, 3]
feedback band, a near-tie (r1 at 10, r2 at 11), q oscillating until 20, A0 rising at 18: both A1 (19) and A2 (20)
rise in one server transaction; H_ARB holds in B's strong form `ArbOK2` for T ≥ 7. My check
(`DoubleAckFrozen.lean`): the same waveforms meet the FROZEN `ArbOK` (requests only) for every T ≥ 9, and fail it at
T = 8 (negative control). So under the frozen H_ARB too, H_ARB(T) does not give mutual exclusion: safety needs the
derived obligation "server path from the last pin edge to A0's rise ≥ T + W" (B's `one_ack`). That is a legitimate
derived obligation (PROGRAM: cell contracts must be derived), not a defect of H_ARB per se; together with 2.4 it
fixes a ceiling on T. Not shown by B or me: that the target's arbiters can see such a near-tie under the bands (B's
unit-delay sims saw request rises at least 36 ticks apart). Verdict: CONFIRMED (in B's model; extends to the frozen
ArbOK).
