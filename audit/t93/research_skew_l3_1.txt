  The cause is placement, every time.  On gcd this site's own link spends 900 ps
  getting from its C node to its OWN latch's enable -- two LUTs of one cell --
  and another 1050 ps reaching the mux, while the first element of the same
  link's delay chain sits 150 ps away.  Nothing declares that net critical, so
  the placer, minimising wirelength over the whole design, has no reason to care.
  Padding is therefore a correction, not a cure: it costs latency on every
  transaction through the gate and it changes the very route that was measured.
  Per-link and measured it is worth trying -- a handful of LUT1s perturbs little.
  If the loop does not converge, the answer is to make the placer keep a link's
  C node and its latch in one slice, which is a packing decision, not a pinned
  location.
